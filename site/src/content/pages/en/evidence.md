Every time something breaks I write a line into an append-only record. Writing it down is cheap
and close to useless. A line is only worth something once some file carries it and changes what
happens today: a rule that loads every session, a skill's Known failures section, a check that
runs. The Cited by column in the table below is where I check that, and it is also where the
record turns around and accuses me.


I wrote that line in my own release notes, and I put it at the top of this page rather than the
bottom. The table proves one thing: every rule here came from something that actually broke, and
one click takes you to the file. It does not prove the other thing, that the loop holds up when
real work goes through it.

## orphan

{{meta.cited}} of {{meta.total}} lines have at least one file carrying them. The other
{{meta.orphan}} point nowhere yet. They are observations I recorded that never became anything
binding.

I left them in the table instead of filtering them out, and I am not planning to tidy the number
up. A record that only shows the closed half cannot be used to check yourself, and at that point
it is a sales document. The orphaned half is also the most honest reading of where this system
is still thin: wherever the unbound lines cluster is where I have watched a problem without
fixing it. You see exactly what I see when I open that file.
